### Chilled Water Air Handlers  
*Source id: Ds9LxzhfaVQ*

- Chilled water air handlers use water as the cooling medium in what would be the evaporator coil; they come in every configuration (upflow, pancake, floor-mount commercial, rooftop) and both water pipes are insulated the same size.
- 2-pipe systems switch the whole building between heat and cool; 4-pipe systems run heat and cool simultaneously with separate coils/loops.
- As with DX, the goal is to hold fixed coil/water temperatures (chillers typically 44F supply water) for consistent dehumidification and comfort.

### Do Houses Need to Breathe？ w⧸ Allison Bailes  
*Source id: UTBNNhUH5V8*

- A house does not need to breathe (answered on page 23): build tight and ventilate right instead of relying on random leaks for 'fresh' air.
- Control four separate flows in priority order: liquid water first, then air, then water vapor and heat; confusing liquid water with water vapor, or air with water vapor, is a common source of building failures.
- Indoor air quality is a layered approach: air tightness, source control, moisture control, MERV 13 filtration, and ventilation, in that order.

### Dr. Allison Bailes Discusses His New Book  
*Source id: -BV65NODcUw*

- A house does not need to breathe (answered on page 23 of the book): sealing tight keeps out bad air from moldy crawl spaces, garages, and attics; 'people need to breathe.'
- Indoor air quality is a five-part layered approach: air tightness, source control, moisture control, MERV 13 filtration, and whole-house mechanical ventilation, in that order.
- You can get MERV 13 filtration at low pressure drop (0.05-0.06 in w.c.) if you size the filter properly (about two square feet per ton) for low face velocity.

### Eugene Silberstein and Jason Obrzut from ESCO have a Conversation with Bert  
*Source id: 3Yei8T32oNA*

- The pressure-enthalpy (P-H) chart, though intimidating, can be broken into component parts and used as a visual troubleshooting tool — you can identify causes of system failures by how the chart shifts, without doing a bunch of calculations.
- The industry is in a mandated (federal regulation) transition to new low-GWP refrigerants that brings changes to equipment, tanks, and service practices; it's already in effect in some states (e.g., Florida).

### Industry Nerd Out w⧸ Ross Trethewey and Bryan from IBS  
*Source id: o2WLncKP29E*

- Hybrid VRF/HVRF systems use water as the heat-transfer medium (hydronics) combined with heat-pump technology, enabling simultaneous heating/cooling and heat recovery to domestic hot water.
- Demand-control ventilation driven by IAQ sensors (CO2, radon, PM, VOCs) with low-speed continuous ECM fans that boost on demand ventilates only as needed, saving energy.
- Modern inverter/EVI heat pumps have overturned old bad reputations and work well even at very low temperatures.

## Contrarian takes (where Bryan / guests diverge from common teaching)

- **Common teaching:** A house needs to breathe / shouldn't be sealed too tight.
  **Bryan's position:** (via Bailes) No house does not need to breathe; you want it as airtight as possible and then ventilate right.
  **Reasoning:** Air pulled through random leaks comes from moldy crawl spaces, garages (gasoline/pesticides), and attics ('dead squirrel on the top plate'), so it isn't fresh; assemblies need to be able to dry out, which is a different statement than 'breathe.'
  **Source:** [Do Houses Need to Breathe？ w⧸ Allison Bailes] (id: UTBNNhUH5V8)

- **Common teaching:** Don't seal a house too tight / a MERV 13 filter uses too much energy and is hard on the AC.
  **Bryan's position:** (via Bailes) You want airtight houses, and you can run MERV 13 at low pressure drop; don't believe everything you're told.
  **Reasoning:** Air from random leaks is not fresh, and properly sized MERV 13 (large surface, low face velocity) gives cleaner air without high pressure drop; a lot of passed-down 'rules' were never true.
  **Source:** [Dr. Allison Bailes Discusses His New Book] (id: -BV65NODcUw)

- **Common teaching:** Heat pumps don't work in the cold / there's 'no heat' to move
  **Bryan's position:** There's plenty of heat outside; -13F is warm relative to absolute zero and modern heat pumps work there
  **Reasoning:** Inverter compressors, EVI, ECM motors and better controls changed the game vs 70s-90s heat pumps
  **Source:** [Industry Nerd Out w⧸ Ross Trethewey and Bryan from IBS] (id: o2WLncKP29E)

- **Common teaching:** Duct sizing debates in the industry.
  **Bryan's position:** Ed provocatively maintains that ducts cannot be too big, admitting he enjoys getting a rise out of people with it but insists it is factual in the context he uses it.
  **Reasoning:** He uses it to provoke productive argument, and says in his context the claim is factual and fun.
  **Source:** [Life Chats with Ed Janowiak at the ACCA Booth] (id: UV1w-BG1yHs)

## Diagnostic reasoning chains

**Chilled Water Air Handlers** (id: Ds9LxzhfaVQ)
- Getting readings is the hard part: often there are no test ports (adding them compromises insulation); use enthalpy probes (Testo 605i / UEI) across the coil plus known airflow to estimate BTU removed and compare to specs.
- In multi-tenant/condo buildings a tech may be told chilled water is being delivered when a chiller is actually offline; verify the water temperature is in range yourself.

## Field tips (the trick that saves time)

- Pipe the 2- or 3-way valve over the unit drain pan (or an added sloped pan) so any condensate from imperfect insulation drips into the pan, not the building.  *(id: Ds9LxzhfaVQ)*
- Use 2-way valves on variable-flow (VFD pump) systems and 3-way on constant-volume pump systems (3-way on supply = diverting, on return = mixing; mixing is often preferred).  *(id: Ds9LxzhfaVQ)*
- Don't swap 3-way valves for 2-way without variable-flow capability; keep some 3-way valves at the ends of loops/branches to keep water mixing and avoid stagnant, temperature-drifting zones.  *(id: Ds9LxzhfaVQ)*
- Bleed the coil's air bleed a few turns to confirm the coil isn't air-bound and has water pressure; don't back it all the way out (water is under pressure).  *(id: Ds9LxzhfaVQ)*
- Actuators (separate from the valve) set on/off vs modulating: 2 wires = spring return, 4+ wires = variable/proportional.  *(id: Ds9LxzhfaVQ)*
- Chilled water pairs well with VAV because 44F water can't freeze the coil, so any fan speed is safe.  *(id: Ds9LxzhfaVQ)*
- Coordinate with building maintenance and know the pump and makeup-water shutoffs before working on valves; a broken valve on a high-flow water system is a major flood.  *(id: Ds9LxzhfaVQ)*
- You can run MERV 13 at low pressure drop if you size the filter for low face velocity (enlarge the filter surface).  *(id: UTBNNhUH5V8)*
- Most water vapor moving through a wall rides with air leakage, so controlling airflow controls most vapor flow; you don't always need a vapor barrier (interior poly in the Southeast will rot the wall).  *(id: UTBNNhUH5V8)*
- Source control ('remove the pile of manure') beats trying to ventilate a problem away.  *(id: UTBNNhUH5V8)*
- Address the obvious/basic IAQ items (air tightness, source control, moisture, filtration) before jumping to fancy ventilation machinery.  *(id: -BV65NODcUw)*
- Keep learning; codes increasingly require blower-door testing and houses are getting tighter.  *(id: -BV65NODcUw)*
- Understand the 'why' behind the trade, not just 'you see this, you do this' — knowing the physics keeps you engaged and makes you a better troubleshooter.  *(id: 3Yei8T32oNA)*
- In humid markets, bringing in outdoor air is a net negative unless dehumidified; latent load dominates design.  *(id: o2WLncKP29E)*
- True theme: personal/community 'life chat' with Ed Janowiak at the ACCA booth; minimal technical content.  *(id: UV1w-BG1yHs)*
- ACCA's own event is held in Orlando at Universal's Sapphire Falls.  *(id: UV1w-BG1yHs)*
