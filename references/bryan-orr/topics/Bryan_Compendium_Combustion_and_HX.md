### A Steam Heat Primer by Dan Holohan  
*Source id: rnVOwUtr15M*

### Adjusting Gas Pressure on a Standard and Gemini Furnace Valve  
*Source id: d1L6Ut2pIYI*

- Adjusting gas pressure is a professional-only task that must be paired with clocking the gas meter (input) and combustion analysis to confirm you're not over/under firing.
- Standard single-stage valve: shut off gas, remove inlet and outlet (manifold) plugs, connect a manometer reading P1 (inlet) and P2 (manifold/outlet); turn the brass adjustment screw clockwise to INCREASE manifold pressure.
- The White-Rodgers Gemini two-stage valve uses different ports (loosen the small Allen screws a couple turns, don't remove them; gas leaks around the screw) and has separate low-fire and high-fire adjustments under brass caps.

### Advanced Combustion Analysis (HVAC) w⧸ Jim Bergmann  
*Source id: RpC2hehz6Og*

- Clock the gas meter to set input for your AVERAGE local heat content (which varies minute-to-minute) - never set a furnace by adjusting gas pressure to a target excess-air on the analyzer (Wobbe index / interchangeability issue).
- Getting the input correct is worth ~4-7% on gas long-term; on 90%+ furnaces incorrect (high) input causes excess air that dries out flue gases so the appliance won't condense (no condensate = a problem).
- CO is a hockey stick: everything can look good at zero ppm, then crossing ~0.5% oxygen sends CO through the roof - watch excess air/oxygen and remember combustion efficiency is NOT appliance efficiency (AFUE).

### Combustion Air Myths W⧸ David Richardson  
*Source id: 2T0mkFJMR2I*

- Draft is the force that controls combustion air; as draft goes up, combustion air increases, and as draft goes down, combustion air decreases. Temperature difference (heating the air), not pressure, is the number-one driver of draft/stack effect.
- A flue is a duct and the building itself is a duct system; four rules govern combustion air: air takes the path of least resistance, one CFM out means one CFM in, higher pressure moves to lower pressure, and hot air rises while cooler air falls to displace it.
- You cannot verify combustion air with a tape measure and code book; you must measure with a combustion analyzer, a draft gauge, and a micromanometer, and mechanical (fan) combustion air is repeatable while natural/passive is controlled by mother nature.

### Combustion Analysis and Troubleshooting with Tyler Nelson  
*Source id: 62FaincBpjU*

- A combustion analysis is a snapshot in time (like blood work) that documents the state of the appliance the day you were there and protects you from liability; the analyzer is your number-one tool, not a tape measure.
- Use CO air-free (CO with the oxygen removed) when actually doing combustion analysis - it is your 'hell number'; use regular CO for ambient monitoring/personal protection. About 85% of installed equipment needs adjustment because it is built in lab conditions.
- The simplest adjustment (gas pressure) has the greatest downstream effect - it changes oxygen, CO, NOx, efficiency, and longevity; always verify gas pressure (the heartbeat) before running combustion analysis (the blood work).

### Combustion Analysis w⧸ Jim Bergmann  
*Source id: JxFOzQz8WzA*

- Get the input correct before you combustion-test: check ambient CO, start and check for spillage, set fuel pressure to spec, then clock the gas meter using the average heat content of the fuel and adjust fuel pressure only within 3.2-3.8 in wc to hit correct input; if you can't, change the orifices.
- The only two adjustments on most modern furnaces are fuel pressure and blower speed; a combustion analyzer measures combustion efficiency, not appliance AFUE (they converge on condensing appliances after standby losses are eliminated).
- Fixate on CO air-free (safety, <100 ppm achievable, 400 ppm stack limit) and excess air (25-50% typical); excess air and fuel pressure move inversely because the draft-inducer fan moves constant CFM.

### Condensing Furnace Drain Guidance w⧸ AC Service Tech  
*Source id: yC-dD-9AX3c*

- A 90%+ (condensing) furnace has a secondary heat exchanger where the cooler conditioned air contacts it first, condensing water out of the flue gas to extract extra latent heat; the condensate is acidic (pH ~3-5) and can eat PVC, metal drain assemblies, and copper.
- Condensate drain freezing is a real cold-climate problem; solutions include a 1/4-inch (or code-minimum 1/8-inch) per foot pitch with no bellies, dripping a 3/4-inch line into a larger 1.5-2 inch pipe with an air gap, condensate pumps (which create an air gap and can include an acid-neutralizer cartridge), or heat tape.
- Codes on where furnace condensate can drain vary hugely by AHJ and are often written to avoid responsibility; know the appliance well enough to install per manufacturer instructions and keep it safe for the occupant.

### Do you even CAZ test？ w⧸ Jim Bergmann  
*Source id: cab4uz3BnYA*

- CAZ (combustion air zone) worst-case draft and pressure testing checks whether enough combustion air exists so the appliance can vent/burn safely; you drive the room to its worst-case negative pressure and confirm no draft-hood spillage.
- Just because an install is code-compliant doesn't mean it works: blocked/removed combustion-air pipes, disconnected supply ducts pressurizing the attic, central returns, wood stoves, and big kitchen exhausts can all depressurize the CAZ and back-draft appliances.
- Measuring draft requires a high-resolution/accurate manometer (Pascals / thousandths of inches w.c.); resolution, precision, and accuracy are three different things and gauge specs are often games (percent of full-scale vs percent of reading).

### Flame Sensor Troubleshooting & Install  
*Source id: epJ_ZThACRM*

- A flame sensor senses current (microamps) through the flame to ground; a poor ground/neutral connection (rust, loose neutral terminal) blocks that current and throws a flame-sensor fault when the real problem is grounding — check ground-to-neutral continuity first.
- The rod rectifies the AC flame current to a DC voltage pulse (hence 'flame rectifier'); the board reads that DC signal to confirm flame. Test with the meter on microamps (DC), not a clamp, because the current is too small for a magnetic-field clamp.
- Positioning matters: the replacement rod must sit at the exact angle/reach of the original so the tip is in the flame; use a universal flame sensor with the manufacturer's chart/app to match part number, bend angle, and whether to cut.

### Furnaces - Introduction and History w⧸ Jim Bergmann  
*Source id: LPOHKjgb9Rs*

- Combustion efficiency (~80%) has barely changed since 1930; modern AFUE gains came from eliminating standby losses (draft hood, standing pilot) and, in condensing furnaces, extracting the latent heat of the water vapor in the flue gas.
- Old furnaces transferred heat by radiation using a carbon-luminous (orange/gold) flame; a bright blue flame in an old radiant furnace would nearly stop heat transfer and send heat up the chimney. Modern furnaces switched to convection heat transfer with a blue flame and complex heat exchangers.
- Excess air is required for safe combustion but wastes energy; on a condensing furnace the input and excess air must be trimmed correctly or the dilution/dry air prevents the flue gas from condensing and kills the rated efficiency.

### Gas Appliances - Preparing for Heating Season Part 1  
*Source id: Q1RC5GKY3iY*

- Gas furnace sequence of operation: W (heat) call -> board confirms the inducer pressure switch starts OPEN -> inducer fan starts -> pressure switch must then CLOSE on negative pressure -> safety circuit (daisy-chained) closed -> ignition (hot surface or spark, adaptive timing) -> gas valve opens -> prove flame via flame rectification -> blower starts.
- Flame rectification requires the appliance to be well grounded with a clean flame rod immersed in the flame; corroded pool heaters that ignite then shut off are almost always failing to prove flame because grounding is lost through the corroded burner assembly.
- The inducer creates NEGATIVE pressure at the furnace outlet (stack effect/vacuum from rising hot air), not positive pressure; it is induced draft (providing combustion air), not power venting, and is only needed because modern heat exchangers are complex.

### Gas Appliances - Preparing for Heating Season Part 2  
*Source id: uGxuhdBKRw0*

- Know the four gas ignition types before ordering universal parts: standing pilot (thermocouple), direct spark (sparks the main burner), intermittent spark (sparks a pilot that lights the main), and hot surface ignition. A thermocouple GENERATES a millivolt current that both powers and proves flame; a flame rod generates nothing -- it just conducts current to ground to prove flame.
- A thermocouple must NOT be scoured (you rub off its coating and destroy it), but a flame rod is just a piece of steel that you CAN and should clean to a smooth (not rough) surface, and it must be well immersed in the flame.
- Set gas pressure with a dual-port manometer to the middle of spec (natural gas outlet ~3.3-3.8 in WC, i.e. ~3.5); ALWAYS reinstall the pressure-port plugs before leaving or you make a bomb. Check inlet and outlet with other appliances running, because gas pressure sags under load like water pressure.

### Gas Furnace Class  
*Source id: HY4xseRx4S4*

- Universal gas furnace sequence of operation: W call -> board verifies inducer pressure switch starts OPEN -> inducer fan runs -> pressure switch CLOSES on negative pressure -> safety/limit circuit closed -> igniter (standing pilot/direct spark/intermittent spark/hot surface) -> gas valve opens -> flame proven by flame rod (or thermocouple on millivolt systems) -> blower starts after 30-90 s.
- Flame proving via flame rectification: the flame ionizes and passes a small DC MICROAMP current from the board to ground; if it won't sense flame it is almost always (a) a dirty rod, (b) a rod not immersed in the flame, or (c) poor grounding -- test by wiring a DC microamp meter in series with the flame rod.
- Set gas pressure with a dual-port manometer measuring inlet and manifold separately (NG ~3.5 in WC; LP ~11 in WC high fire). Inlet can sag under load like water pressure, so check with other gas appliances running; and ALWAYS reinstall the test-port plugs.

### Gas Furnace Class w⧸ Bert  
*Source id: lvZ5iN1xh7Q*

- Learn the furnace sequence of operation cold so you can spot deviations: W call -> board confirms pressure switch OPEN -> inducer runs -> pressure switch CLOSES on vacuum -> safeties closed -> igniter -> gas valve opens -> flame proven within <3 s -> blower after a delay. Ignition retries 3x then locks out.
- Before opening or resetting anything, READ the blinking fault code (long flash = letter, short flashes = number); resetting power erases the diagnostic history you need.
- The pressure switch is wired on the VACUUM side so a blocked flue can't falsely hold it closed; confirm actual airflow with a manometer (needs ~0.3 in WC to pull in) before condemning the switch, and check gas pressure (inlet AND manifold) while the flame is burning.

### Gas Furnace Overview w⧸ AC Service Tech  
*Source id: H7144pxGh7M*

- A 90%+ condensing furnace adds a SECONDARY (tube-and-fin) heat exchanger after the primary; the return air passes over the cooler secondary FIRST, then the hotter primary (counterflow) to pull the last bit of heat out of the flue gas as it condenses. PVC exhaust = 90%+ (near skin temperature, ~0 in clearance to combustibles); metal B-vent ~1 in clearance.
- Flame rectification: a flame rod carries 80-180 V AC (often ~100 V); the flame rectifies it to DC, which you test in series as ~3 MICROamps (not milliamps). The rod 'senses' nothing -- it's just a stainless rod with ceramic isolation and rarely needs replacement.
- Convert NG to propane by swapping the valve springs (silver = NG, white = LP), the orifices, and adjusting combustion air; NG runs ~5-7 in WC inlet / ~3.5 manifold (~1/4 PSI), propane ~10-13 in WC (~1/2 PSI).

### Gas Pool Heater Kalos Meeting w⧸ Bert  
*Source id: mps9rJhJL7A*

- Water flow is the most common gas pool heater problem, not heater component failure -- if water limit switches trip (and it's not just rusted connections), the heater is overheating and you diagnose water flow in and out.
- The safety chain mirrors a furnace: inducer proves airflow (normally-open airflow switch closes on vacuum) -> safeties -> igniter (hot surface or spark/pilot) -> gas valve -> flame proven within 3 seconds via a flame rod that needs to be in the flame AND the heater well grounded.
- Read the data tag for min/max inlet and manifold gas pressure and check both while running; if a heater lights but rolls out or burns dirty, gas pressure is a prime suspect (customers/gas supplier fix regulators, not the tech).

### HVAC Furnace Sequence Of Operation  
*Source id: yVe9aUX8wu4*

- Six-step furnace sequence of operation with a mnemonic 'take it slow, it's gonna blow': Thermostat (24V W call), Inducer motor (120V out), Safety switch (pressure switch proves airflow), Igniter, Gas valve, Blower motor. The board works input/output -- every input triggers an output.
- The pressure ('safety'/air-proving) switch works on very low pressure (0.2-0.5 in WC, some 1-2), so a cracked heat exchanger, a bad inducer gasket, cracked switch tubing, or a drop of condensation in the hose can keep it from closing -- like a straw with a hole in the side. Don't just condemn the switch; hook up a manometer and find WHY.
- There is no G call in gas heat -- the blower comes on only after a timer once flame is proven; a simultaneous G call circulates cold air, can affect venting, and may lock some furnaces out.

### How to Commission a Gas Furnace w/ Jim Bergmann  
*Source id: GI2nairsrIE*

- Take your blinders off and become a 'master of the obvious': gas heating is a SYSTEM (controls, distribution, filtration, condensate, venting, make-up air, fuel delivery, the house), not just the appliance. 'Appliance fixation' is the root of most mistakes.
- You cannot correct what you do not measure: a combustion analyzer (O2, CO, draft/temperature) plus a personal CO monitor are mandatory for gas work; test ambient CO on every floor, in the airstream (supply and return), and in the stack.
- Input is the key to safe operation: set manifold pressure within the acceptable RANGE and clock the meter to verify input, correcting for local gas heat content and altitude; if pressure must fall outside range, change orifices per the manual.

### How to Measure Gas Pressure - Kalos Meeting  
*Source id: xgZQmaE4HFA*

- Sequence for measuring gas pressure: shut off the gas valve, remove inlet/outlet test ports, insert barbed fittings, zero the manometer to atmosphere, run the appliance, then measure inlet AND outlet (manifold) pressure simultaneously.
- Know whether your manometer measures each port independently (referenced to atmosphere) or only the differential between two ports; cheap ones only read differential so you must measure one port at a time.
- Do not adjust the valve on low manifold pressure until you confirm inlet pressure is adequate, because low outlet pressure can be caused by low inlet supply (undersized line) rather than the valve setting.

### Measuring & Adjusting Furnace Gas Pressure X2  
*Source id: rhNn3jYUjWc*

- Gas pressure testing is for trained professionals only: always shut the gas off before removing plugs, get the plugs back in and well-sealed afterward, and check with a combustible gas leak detector plus soap bubbles.
- Turning the gas-valve adjustment screw clockwise increases output (manifold) pressure; counterclockwise decreases it. The visible cap must be removed first — it is not the adjustment.
- On two-stage furnaces there are separate high-fire and low-fire adjustment points; follow the manufacturer's specified order (some say adjust high first then low) or you get funky pressures in the opposite stage.

### Rack Refrigeration Defrost  
*Source id: uQrHabPalpE*

- Three defrost strategies: time-off (medium temp, space above freezing, just move air), electric (heaters for medium/low temp), and gas — hot gas (from the discharge after the oil separator) or liquid/'cool gas' — either way you bring warmer fluid into the coil.
- In gas defrost you close the suction-line solenoid and open the defrost solenoid so gas flows into the suction line, up through the coil, warms it, and returns down the liquid line into the running rack's liquid header.
- Flow during defrost while the rest of the rack runs is created by a differential valve (on the liquid line for hot/cool gas, or the discharge line for hot gas only) running ~20-25 psi differential so the liquid header sits below the receiver/discharge pressure.

### Trane Voyager Gas Screen Tip  
*Source id: TUfW-VdkR8c*

- On Trane Voyager rooftop units, a dirty/clogged combustion screen (held by two of four bolts to the shell) causes the unit to fire, run ~3 seconds, then drop out and lose flame.
- Techs waste money replacing inducers, gas valves, hot surface igniters and limits when the real fix is cleaning that screen; if it's rusted out, the heat exchanger may be too.

### White Rodgers Premium Flame Sensor Intro  
*Source id: HaqWJE_5Z4s*

- A flame rod is not special metal that generates current — the board feeds a small potential and a tiny DC current is conducted to ground through the flame; it's called a rectifier because the size difference between the rod and the large burner assembly changes AC to DC.
- Flame current is measured in microamps by wiring your meter in series with the rod; because the current is so tiny, rods must be kept clean and damaged rods replaced.
- The Emerson White-Rodgers premium flame sensor is universal — bend it or cut it per the color-coded charts to replace over 150 OEM parts (rated to 2200°F, 3-year warranty).

## Canonical field stories

### The elevator that killed the boiler's combustion
- **Setting:** Lorain Metropolitan Housing Authority, ~1 million BTU sealed-combustion beast boiler
- **Diagnosis chain:** graphing analyzer showed O2 swinging wildly and CO spiking >3000 ppm; the elevator going up/down created a plunger/pressure effect (taped-off shaft vent) that overcame the sealed combustion, plus an abandoned chimney at -50 Pascal
- **Root cause:** building pressure dynamics (elevator plunger) overcoming sealed combustion
- **Lesson:** without a graphing combustion analyzer you'd never see it - look at the whole building, not just the appliance
- **Source:** [Advanced Combustion Analysis (HVAC) w⧸ Jim Bergmann] (id: RpC2hehz6Og)

### CO poisoning victim thought she had the flu
- **Setting:** a home Bill and Jim went to
- **Diagnosis chain:** occupant had headaches, dizziness, nausea, fatigue - flu-like symptoms - while high CO filled the home
- **Root cause:** CO source in the home
- **Lesson:** calibrate analyzer outdoors, walk the whole home at chest height (CO mixes evenly, doesn't stratify); don't fixate on the furnace - check water heater, stove, auto emissions
- **Source:** [Advanced Combustion Analysis (HVAC) w⧸ Jim Bergmann] (id: RpC2hehz6Og)

### Colorado Springs combustion-analysis mandate
- **Setting:** family of four; installers replaced a furnace and barely connected the PVC
- **Diagnosis chain:** CO leaked into the father's adjacent office for three months; the CO detectors never went off; his wife noticed cognitive decline and a doctor diagnosed permanent neurological impairment
- **Root cause:** improperly connected PVC exhaust on a new high-efficiency furnace
- **Lesson:** CO detectors alarm too late; test combustion, and that township now makes combustion analysis mandatory on fuel-fired replacements
- **Source:** [Combustion Analysis and Troubleshooting with Tyler Nelson] (id: 62FaincBpjU)

### Merrimack Valley over-pressurization explosion (Sept 14 2018)
- **Setting:** Massachusetts; gas company removed regulators/sensors, delivered gas at over 200 PSI instead of inches of water column
- **Diagnosis chain:** appliances calling for domestic hot water off boilers that day caught fire; one technician's analyzer showed gas pressure of 3.5 in wc, which kept him out of the lawsuit
- **Root cause:** gas delivered at over 200 PSI due to pirated regulators/sensors
- **Lesson:** gas pressure is critical and documenting it protects you; that gas company can no longer practice in MA, RI, CT, PA, OH
- **Source:** [Combustion Analysis and Troubleshooting with Tyler Nelson] (id: 62FaincBpjU)

### Sealed boiler back-drafted by an elevator shaft and abandoned chimney
- **Setting:** 20-30 story building mechanical room
- **Diagnosis chain:** Abandoned very tall chimney (~-50 Pa) plus an elevator shaft depressurized the combustion air zone so much that a sealed-combustion boiler intermittently made huge amounts of CO and tripped its pressure switch.
- **Root cause:** Extreme CAZ depressurization overcame even the sealed appliance's venting.
- **Lesson:** Even sealed-combustion appliances can be affected by room depressurization; measure worst-case CAZ pressure.
- **Source:** [Do you even CAZ test？ w⧸ Jim Bergmann] (id: cab4uz3BnYA)

### CO poisoning from missing base pan + no exhaust accelerator
- **Setting:** Expert-witness case, residential basement with two-stage furnace
- **Diagnosis chain:** Furnace vented in 3-inch PVC with no reducer/exhaust accelerator and low input never filled the condensate trap, sucking flue gas back up the secondary heat exchanger and making CO; the missing furnace base pan let the return depressurize the basement and pull CO back into the home through a ventilated soffit under an ornamental fireplace.
- **Root cause:** Combination of un-sealed bottom base pan (depressurized CAZ), no exhaust accelerator, and low input causing CO with no path out except back into the house.
- **Lesson:** Seal the furnace base pan, install the exhaust accelerator/reducer on two-stage vents, and CAZ-test; a smoke/fog puffer shows air rushing into the depressurized space.
- **Source:** [Do you even CAZ test？ w⧸ Jim Bergmann] (id: cab4uz3BnYA)

### CSA/AGA lab re-meters every furnace before testing

### Live LP furnace commissioning demo
- **Setting:** Classroom furnace running on a propane tank (converted from natural gas)

**Combustion Air Myths W⧸ David Richardson** (id: 2T0mkFJMR2I)
- Rising CO with falling oxygen and low/absent draft is the classic signature of a combustion air problem (flue gases backing up, oxygen not reaching the burner) - take multiple readings over the whole cycle, not one steady-state reading.
- Draft interference test: run the equipment stable, then kick on the biggest blower (furnace) and other exhaust fans and change door positions while watching the Dwyer draft ball; any downward movement of the ball is the fan/condition interfering with combustion air.
- To distinguish a restricted flue from a combustion air problem, shut off the gas while running and watch draft; if the ball shoots up after ~15 seconds the flue is restricted (removing flue-gas heat acts like a relief).

**Combustion Analysis and Troubleshooting with Tyler Nelson** (id: 62FaincBpjU)
- Cracked heat exchanger (forced air, AHRI Guideline X): auto-zero outside in fresh air, run appliance to stable, then when the indoor blower kicks on watch oxygen - an uptick of 2% or greater indicates the negative pressure is drawing air in through a crack (verify 20.9% O2, 0 CO before you start).
- Cross-contamination on a condensing/high-efficiency system: put the probe midway into the fresh-air intake with other fuel-fired appliances running; if you do not read 20.9% oxygen you have cross-contamination (e.g., an exhaust curtain off bushes or a nearby appliance).
- CO spike at light-off (2000 ppm+) suggests delayed ignition/igniter/thermocouple; O2 spike when the fan comes on suggests a gasket or heat-exchanger crack; CO spike at shutdown suggests a leaky gas valve.

**Combustion Analysis w⧸ Jim Bergmann** (id: JxFOzQz8WzA)
- Three-part cracked-heat-exchanger inspection: visual inspection, flame-disruption test (flame flutters when blower kicks on), and a chemical test - watch O2 and CO stay stable when the blower starts/stops because air blows around, not through, the heat exchanger; a downstream crack lets combustion air enter after the burner and CO rises.
- Alternative crack test: shut off gas, disconnect and tape the intake/exhaust, run the blower with a Dwyer 460 draft gauge in the flue; if the ball lifts, air is leaking into the heat exchanger.
- Order of commissioning: input first, then draft, then temperature rise (set by blower speed toward the middle of the range), then re-verify the combustion appliance zone, because changing fuel pressure or blower speed changes everything downstream.

**Do you even CAZ test？ w⧸ Jim Bergmann** (id: cab4uz3BnYA)
- CAZ test sequence: measure CAZ pressure w.r.t. outdoors with door open -> turn on air handler and re-measure (finds duct/base-pan leakage) -> close all interior doors and re-measure (central-return depressurization) -> recreate the combination producing the highest negative pressure -> compare to the minimum required draft for the outdoor temperature and confirm no draft-hood spillage with a mirror/smoke pen/fogger.

**Flame Sensor Troubleshooting & Install** (id: epJ_ZThACRM)
- Furnace pulls a flame-sensor error code -> before condemning the rod, verify the furnace is cleanly grounded to the same neutral as the transformer (check ground-to-neutral connection); only then test whether the board is sending current via microamp test.
- Do a continuity (end-to-end) test on the removed rod to confirm it isn't cracked or internally damaged before deciding it just needs cleaning.

**Furnaces - Introduction and History w⧸ Jim Bergmann** (id: LPOHKjgb9Rs)
- Rusty 80% (non-condensing) furnace -> suspect either condensation occurring inside it (improper setup / insufficient draft) or corrosive chemicals near it (chlorine, bleach, dryer sheets)
- Furnace flame rolls out the front / rollout switch trips -> flue path to the heat exchanger is blocked and the flame is seeking oxygen (like a backdraft)

**Gas Appliances - Preparing for Heating Season Part 1** (id: Q1RC5GKY3iY)
- Pool heater ignites, proves flame, then shuts off repeatedly -> flame rectification failure, usually lost grounding from a corroded burner assembly -> condemn as unsafe and quote replacement
- Inducer pressure switch won't close -> do NOT start with the switch; inspect the flue for blockage (bird nest, condensation/water, kinked or broken tube)
- Flame becomes disturbed/orange when the blower starts -> leakage between the blower air and combustion air, a possible cracked heat exchanger
- CO complaints in a Florida home -> more often the water heater (improper flue + space depressurization causing backdraft) than the rarely-run furnace; inspect the water heater draft-hood top for scorching/moisture

**Gas Appliances - Preparing for Heating Season Part 2** (id: uGxuhdBKRw0)
- Water heater on an oversized flue (orphaned after a furnace was removed) -> flue never gets warm enough to draft -> backdrafting -> high CO; blackened/melted draft hood is the first tell
- Intermittent gas pressure problems -> run every gas appliance at once and watch whether inlet (and therefore outlet) pressure drops under load
- High ambient CO during operation -> suspect exhaust recirculation (sidewall/roof exhaust pulled back through soffit or a fresh-air intake) or space depressurization

**Gas Furnace Class** (id: HY4xseRx4S4)
- Flame lights then furnace shuts off + blinky code for no flame -> flame rectification failure: dirty rod, rod not in flame, or bad grounding (common on rusted-out pool heaters)
- High limit trips -> furnace running too hot -> low airflow: check dirty filter, dirty/iced evaporator coil, blower tap too low; confirm via temperature rise test
- Flame wiggles/goes orange when blower starts (panel ON) -> cracked heat exchanger OR another air leak between the combustion zone and blower air

**Gas Furnace Class w⧸ Bert** (id: lvZ5iN1xh7Q)
- Limit/rollout lockout (open >3 min) -> ask what caused overheating: dirty filter, dirty blower wheel, partially blocked flue (mud daubers), or a flame rollout; recreate the condition rather than just resetting
- Failure-to-prove-ignition code (flame lights fine) -> flame sensor issue: dirty rod, cracked porcelain grounding out, damaged wire, or poor furnace grounding (check neutral-to-ground); confirm board output with a DC microamp test
- Pressure switch won't close -> verify airflow with a manometer (>=0.3 in WC); if airflow is present it's the switch or board, if not check flue blockage / inducer / kinked hose / water in hose

**Gas Furnace Overview w⧸ AC Service Tech** (id: H7144pxGh7M)
- Flame flickers/dances when the blower starts -> likely cracked heat exchanger (or a plate pulled away from it); confirm by camera through the limit-switch opening or coil access, and by reading CO at the supply registers
- Repeatedly tripping flame rollout switch -> never just keep resetting; blower air is pushing flame back, from a cracked heat exchanger, a plate pulled from the exchanger, or improper combustion air
- Condensate pressure switch open / 'block drain' -> clogged condensate trap backing water into the collector box/inducer; clear the trap with air or a hydraulic water flush

**Gas Pool Heater Kalos Meeting w⧸ Bert** (id: mps9rJhJL7A)
- Heater won't light + error code -> confirm gas is supplied and the igniter actually glows red; on a recently refilled/idle propane tank, air in the lines can fail the first 3 tries, so reset and retry to purge the air
- Overheat/stack-flue trip -> check the flue and blower wheel for critters/blockage; the stack flue sensor is just a temperature probe, so test it against a known temperature via the board display before condemning it
- Repeated flame-proving failure on a rusted heater -> suspect lost grounding (rusted-out bottom / corroded ground) preventing flame-rectification current

**HVAC Furnace Sequence Of Operation** (id: yVe9aUX8wu4)
- Pressure switch won't close -> manometer the port; check for cracked heat exchanger, bad inducer gasket, cracked switch tubing, a bird/blockage, or a drop of condensation in the hose
- Flame-disruption test when the blower starts -> only valid with the door panel ON and all other 'straws' eliminated (coil gaps, drilled/un-sealed holes, nearby vents); on high-eff use combustion analysis (watch O2/CO2 shift) instead

**How to Commission a Gas Furnace w/ Jim Bergmann** (id: GI2nairsrIE)
- AHRI induced-draft heat exchanger inspection order: 4.1 look for flame disturbances -> 4.2 measure CO in the airstream (supply and return) -> 4.3 measure CO in the stack -> 4.4 verify proper installation (owner's manual venting requirements) -> 4.5 visually inspect the heat exchanger by removal only if the other steps fail.
- Zero the analyzer in fresh air (all readings are relative to a baseline) -> test ambient CO chest-high on every floor -> then supply/return airstream -> then stack, measured CO air-free to remove dilution effect.
- Temperature rise: close a nearby supply and return, measure 6-10 ft from the furnace where air has mixed; steady state = the delta-T across the heat exchanger stabilizes (stack temp keeps creeping up with return-air temp).

**How to Measure Gas Pressure - Kalos Meeting** (id: xgZQmaE4HFA)
- Intermittent gas furnace problems: run all gas appliances in the house simultaneously, then check gas pressure, because inlet pressure can drop under full household load causing rollout and other issues.
- Wiggly flame when blower starts -> air leaking into the combustion chamber from the air side (usually easily fixed); worst case is a heat exchanger crack.

**Leading With Intent At The Sauermann Booth** (id: Df2kZqwfDbE)
- Insert the analyzer probe midway into the fresh air intake (about 6-8 inches up, roughly parallel to the combustion test spot) -> read percentage of oxygen -> if it is not 20.9% you have cross contamination from the appliance, an adjacent appliance, or a neighbor's appliance.

**Rack Refrigeration Defrost** (id: uQrHabPalpE)
- Identify hot vs cool gas defrost by tracing the defrost header to its source — if it tees off the discharge line right after the oil separator, it's hot gas defrost.
- The differential valve establishes flow: it lowers the liquid-header pressure below the receiver so discharge (higher) pushes gas back from the high side to the liquid header during defrost while other circuits keep cooling.

**Trane Voyager Gas Screen Tip** (id: TUfW-VdkR8c)
- Unit fires, runs ~3 seconds, flames go out repeatedly -> inspect and clean the combustion screen and confirm it is mounted back into the screw holes rather than left out or broken.

**White Rodgers Premium Flame Sensor Intro** (id: HaqWJE_5Z4s)
- If you find a rod getting dirty often, don't just clean it — check the flame quality with a combustion analysis and check for chemicals in the space mixing with combustion air causing premature fouling.
