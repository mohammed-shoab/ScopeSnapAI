⚠️ SAFETY Fast-Track
🧡 Bryan Orr — from the 960-transcript archive
"SnapAI has real diagnostic reasoning on the electrical + refrigerant faults. What you don't have is airflow diagnosis — the actual most-common problem in residential HVAC per two independent DOE studies. Static pressure isn't in your reading types. CFM isn't measured. Cracked heat exchanger isn't a card. A junior tech running SnapAI today gets senior-tech-quality output on maybe 40% of the calls he takes. Fix the Tier 1 gaps and that jumps to 60-70%. That's when the 80% claim actually holds up on the messy real calls, not just the textbook ones."
Grounded in: 598 duct episodes · 468 compressor · 361 refrigeration · 263 vacuum · 212 airflow · 193 superheat · 152 dehumidif · 87 heat exchanger + more.
⚠️ Safety Fast-Track — Build This Out of Order
The one gap Bryan named as a legal-liability exposure. Ship first regardless of the rest of the sequence.
⚠️ Card #21 — Heat Exchanger Damage (Carbon Monoxide Risk)
The gap: SnapAI has Card #11 (Ignitor / Flame Sensor) for combustion-side no-heat, but no card for cracked heat exchanger detection. Adam Mufich (Duct Renovations episode) named cracked HX as a consequence of chronic low airflow — very common on Houston attic-mount 15+ year gas furnaces. A junior tech running SnapAI on a rusted-out 18-year-old furnace with restricted airflow gets zero HX inspection prompt and zero CO check. That's carbon-monoxide-poisoning liability sitting in your product today.

The fix: Add Card #21 — Heat Exchanger Damage. Force HX inspection prompt on any gas furnace ≥ 12 years old OR any furnace where static pressure exceeds the design budget. Add optional combustion-analyzer readings (CO ppm, O2%, flue temp) for shops with the tool.

Evidence weight: 87 heat exchanger + 37 carbon monoxide + 86 combustion + 42 combustion analyzer episodes across the 960-transcript archive.

Priority: Ship before Tier 1 sequence. This is not about the 80% claim — this is about not being sued.
📏 New Reading Types
Currently live: 8 reading types (uF, amps compressor/blower, voltage L1+L2, voltage drop, delta_T, ohms, micro_amps, temp_delta, PSI suction/discharge). Bryan gap analysis identifies 8 new reading types the app must collect to reach senior-tech-quality diagnosis on the failure modes that matter.
Superheat
🆕 NEWTIER 1
Suction line temperature at compressor + saturation temp from pressure. Load-bearing reading for confirming refrigerant charge diagnosis. Target: 8-12°F at compressor for TXV systems; 10-20°F evap superheat for orifice.
Current gap: Not Cooling YES commits to "Refrigerant Leak" from suction PSI alone. Without superheat, indistinguishable from TXV restriction or low airflow.
Enables: Card #8 refinement, TXV/EEV diagnosis (Card #15 expansion), compression ratio problem detection.
Archive weight: 193 episodes touch superheat.
Bryan (verbatim, AC Pressures class): "our suction saturation our evaporator temperature tells us the temperature of the evaporator and our super heat tells us how full it is. You can't just know one or the other because that doesn't tell you enough."
Subcool
🆕 NEWTIER 1
Liquid line temperature at condenser outlet + condensing saturation temp. Tells us liquid quality at the metering device. Target: 8-15°F depending on manufacturer.
Current gap: Card #17 Overcharge is triggered by "high suction + high discharge" but the more reliable overcharge signal is subcool > 15°F.
Enables: Card #17 refinement, TXV pre-requisite validation, flash-gas-at-metering-device detection.
Archive weight: 113 episodes touch subcool/subcooling.
Bryan (verbatim): "We need to feed our metering device with a full column of liquid full stop. More sub cool ≠ more efficiency — it usually means you're increasing your condensing temperature and shrinking your effective condenser area."
Total External Static Pressure (TESP) + 4-point profile
🆕 NEWTIER 1
Static pressure measured at 4 points: before/after filter, before/after coil. Identifies which component is eating the pressure budget. Design budget for most residential = 0.5 in.w.c.
Current gap: ZERO airflow measurement in SnapAI. DOE meta-analysis (44 studies): 50-93% of residential systems move below 350 CFM/ton.
Enables: Card #20 (new: Under-Airflow), Card #21 Heat Exchanger trigger, oversizing detection (Card #24), coil dirt vs undersized filter differentiation.
Archive weight: 169 static pressure + 212 airflow + 70 manometer episodes — 451+ combined weight.
Bryan: "A 16x25x1 undersized filter can triple the allowed pressure budget on its own. This is the single most powerful fix that's also the most underused."
Micron Gauge + Standing Vacuum Decay
🆕 NEWTIER 1
Micron reading during evacuation + standing decay test (does vacuum rise fast? = leak or moisture). Target: hold at ≤ 500 microns for 15 minutes with rise ≤ 100 microns.
Current gap: Card #8 Leak card description says "pull vacuum to 500 microns" but no verification the tech did it. Silent-compounding failure: recharge over moisture → TXV acid formation 6 months later.
Enables: Validation gate on Cards #8, #10, #12, #15 — no charge step allowed until micron reading + decay test pass.
Archive weight: 101 micron + 263 vacuum + 120 recovery = 484 combined weight.
Bryan: Multiple full episodes with Jim Bergmann (Advanced Evacuation, Evacuation 101, Vacuum Deep Dive) — the industry authority on this.
Wet Bulb / Relative Humidity
🆕 NEWTIER 2
Return air wet-bulb + indoor RH. Distinguishes sensible cooling deficit from latent (humidity) deficit. Houston-critical — a system passing every SnapAI check can still fail on humidity control.
Current gap: No latent-load diagnostic path. No "clammy / sticky" complaint routing.
Enables: Card #22 (new: Latent Capacity Deficit), Comfort Complaint flow.
Archive weight: 152 dehumidification + 97 latent + 94 sensible + 55 wet-bulb.
Bryan (AC Pressures class): "In our market [Houston] we had conditions that [Bergmann] just wasn't used to seeing — these very high latent conditions drive the DTD differently."
Winding-to-Ground Resistance
🆕 NEWTIER 1
Megger or ohmmeter reading from compressor terminals (C, R, S) to compressor housing. Detects grounded winding. Healthy = OL (infinite). Faulty = any measurable resistance.
Current gap: Card #10 Compressor Failure doesn't differentiate grounded vs mechanical. Roman Baugh's live-call episode: cool ambient causes refrigerant migration → intermittent readings that look grounded but aren't.
Enables: Card #10a Grounded Compressor sub-card (part of the Compressor Family expansion).
Archive weight: 55 grounded compressor episodes.
Bryan (Roman Baugh Deep End episode): "Cool ambient temperatures that enable liquid refrigerant to migrate into the compressor could potentially skew electrical readings. Intermittent resistance readings don't always indicate a failed winding."
Combustion Analyzer (CO ppm, O2%, Flue Temp)
⚠️ SAFETY🆕 NEWTIER 1
Testo/Bacharach combustion analyzer readings on gas furnaces. Detects incomplete combustion, cracked heat exchanger CO leaks, misadjusted gas pressure. Target: CO in flue < 100 ppm air-free.
Current gap: Card #11 Ignitor/Flame Sensor covers no-heat symptoms only. No combustion analysis. No CO reading.
Enables: Card #21 Heat Exchanger Damage detection (SAFETY fast-track).
Archive weight: 42 combustion analyzer + 86 combustion + 37 carbon monoxide episodes.
Instrument Confidence Check
🆕 NEWTIER 2
Optional per-reading toggle: gauge last calibrated? clamp position + AC/DC? IR emissivity? Manometer auto-zeroed? Downgrades "High Confidence" label when input confidence is flagged.
Current gap: SnapAI weights junior-tech readings equal to senior-tech readings. Eric Kaiser: stacked inaccuracies (instrument error × density correction error) can push diagnosis in wrong direction.
Enables: Confidence label calibration Bryan already flagged in prior evaluation.
Archive weight: Eric Kaiser's airflow-testing episode is the definitive one; 30 temperature-rise, 42 combustion analyzer, 70 manometer episodes all touch calibration/accuracy.
🎴 New Fault Cards + Expansions
Currently live: 19 fault cards (Card #1 through #19). Bryan analysis adds 5 new cards (Cards #20-24) plus expands Card #10 into a 5-member Compressor Family and Card #15 into a 4-member Metering Device Family.
Card #21 — Heat Exchanger Damage
⚠️ SAFETY🆕 NEW
Trigger: Any gas furnace ≥ 12 years old OR any furnace with TESP > design budget OR flame roll-out reported.
Inputs: Visual HX photo + combustion analyzer CO reading + flue temp.
Escalate condition: CO > 100 ppm air-free OR visible crack → immediate red-tag protocol.
Recommendation: Replace furnace (HX repair is not economically viable on residential furnaces).
Archive weight: 87 HX + 37 CO + 42 combustion analyzer.
Card #20 — System Under-Airflow
🆕 NEWTIER 1
Trigger: TESP > 0.5 in.w.c. OR CFM < 350 per ton (measured or temp-rise calculated).
Differentiate from Card #14 (Dirty Coil): Card #14 fires on dirty photo + high discharge. Card #20 fires on high static across the whole airflow path with no single dirty component.
Differentiate from Card #13 (Ductwork Leak): Card #13 is normal pressure + warm supply (leak losing air to attic). Card #20 is normal supply temp but low CFM (restriction anywhere in the path).
Recommendation: Static pressure profile → identify highest-drop component → filter upsize / coil clean / duct rework.
Archive weight: 598 duct + 212 airflow + 169 static pressure + 87 oversizing (a common cause).
Card #22 — Latent Capacity Deficit
🆕 NEWTIER 2
Trigger: Indoor RH > 60% while sensible cooling meets set point (Houston "clammy" complaint).
Root causes: Oversized equipment short-cycling (never runs long enough to condense water) · Ventilation load (fresh-air intake) · Duct leak on return-side pulling in humid attic air · Missing standalone dehumidifier.
Recommendation: Downsizing analysis, blower speed adjustment (lower CFM → colder coil → more latent removal), dedicated dehumidifier.
Archive weight: 152 dehumidification + 97 latent + 94 sensible + 55 wet-bulb.
Card #23 — Thermostat / Low-Voltage Fault
🆕 NEWTIER 3
Trigger: Not Turning On + no error code + power to control board present, but no call to Y.
Sub-modes: Missing C-wire (smart thermostat drop-outs) · Phantom voltage on control wire · Zone board / staging board failure · Nest/Ecobee compatibility issue with older equipment · Thermostat internal fault.
Differentiate from Card #7 (Control Board): Card #7 is board-side fault. Card #23 is thermostat-side / control-wire fault.
Archive weight: 261 thermostat + 14 zoning episodes.
Card #24 — System Oversizing
🆕 NEWTIER 3
Trigger: Short-cycling pattern (Intermittent Shutdown) + delta-T ≥ 22°F + poor humidity control + Manual J shows unit is 30%+ over calculated load.
Differentiate from Card #18 (Undersizing): Opposite direction; more common per DOE data (up to 93% oversized).
Recommendation: Load calculation → equipment downsizing on next replacement + short-term blower speed reduction.
Archive weight: 87 oversizing + 43 Manual J episodes. Adam Mufich: "A 50% oversized AC system can increase energy consumption by up to 91%."
Card #10 Family — Compressor Sub-Modes (5 members)
🆕 EXPANDTIER 1
Currently Card #10 is a single "Compressor Failure" catch-all. Expand to 5 discriminated sub-modes:

Card #10a — Grounded Compressor: Winding-to-ground resistance test, ambient-temp captured to flag refrigerant migration risk.
Card #10b — Mechanical / Locked Rotor: Amp draw at start attempt, hard-start kit test.
Card #10c — Compression Ratio Problem: Requires superheat + subcool + amp pattern; distinguishes high discharge (dirty condenser / overcharge) from internal valve wear.
Card #10d — Start Component Failure: Hard-start kit, start capacitor, potential relay — each different fix.
Card #10e — Crankcase Heater Failure: Causes slugging on startup; visible on migration-heavy climates in cool ambient.

Why: All 5 currently flatten to "replace compressor" which is a $2-4K wrong call on modes a–e sub 4 of 5.
Archive weight: 468 compressor + 55 grounded + 33 hard start + 29 start capacitor + 24 crankcase heater.
Card #15 Family — Metering Device Sub-Modes (4 members)
🆕 EXPANDTIER 2
Currently Card #15 is "Fixed Orifice / Piston Mismatch" only. Expand to 4 modes:

Card #15a — Piston Mismatch (current — orifice systems only).
Card #15b — TXV Bulb Loss: Bulb charge escaped, valve stuck open, superheat drops to 0-3°F, flooding evap.
Card #15c — TXV Hunting: Oscillating superheat, bulb overcharge or misplacement.
Card #15d — EEV Controller Failure: Won't step, error code on controller, no expansion.

Prerequisite: Requires superheat + subcool readings (Tier 1 gap 2).
Archive weight: 162 TXV + 68 EEV + 24 electronic leak + 5 TXV bulb episodes.

🌳 New / Expanded Complaint Flows
Currently live: 9 complaint types (Not Cooling, Not Heating, Water Dripping, Not Turning On, Making Noise, High Electric Bill, Error Code, Intermittent Shutdown, Service/Tune-Up). Bryan gap analysis adds 4 new complaint entry points + expands 3 existing flows.
🆕 Comfort Complaint (Clammy / Sticky / Uneven Rooms)
🆕 NEWTIER 2
Missing today: Houston homeowner says "system runs but house feels clammy" → tech runs SnapAI → everything reads OK → tech leaves confused.

New flow:
Q1: Which comfort issue? [clammy/sticky | some rooms cold, some rooms hot | dust / air quality]
Q2 clammy: Read return-air wet-bulb + indoor RH.
Q3 clammy: Read blower speed setting + Manual J vs installed capacity check.
→ Routes to Card #22 (Latent Capacity Deficit) or Card #24 (Oversizing) or Card #13 (Ductwork Leak on return side)
Archive weight: 152 dehumidification episodes — dominant Houston-specific concern.
🆕 Airflow Assessment (Cross-Cutting)
🆕 NEWTIER 1
Not a new complaint category — a required sub-flow triggered from multiple existing complaints:
Trigger from: Not Cooling YES (high discharge) · High Electric Bill (both branches) · Intermittent Shutdown · Comfort Complaint.

Flow:
Step 1: Enter Total External Static Pressure (TESP).
Step 2: If TESP > 0.5 in.w.c., collect 4-point profile (before/after filter, before/after coil).
Step 3: Route to highest-drop component → Card #2 (filter) OR Card #14 (coil) OR Card #20 (system under-airflow) OR Card #13 (duct restriction).
Step 4: If TESP OK but comfort/bill complaint persists → route to Card #24 (Oversizing).
Archive weight: 598+212+169+70 = 1049 combined episode weight on airflow / static / manometer topics.
🆕 Vacuum Validation (Cross-Cutting)
🆕 NEWTIER 1
Not a new complaint — a required verification step injected before "charge system" action on all refrigerant-circuit repair cards (#8 Leak, #10 Compressor family, #12 Reversing Valve, #15 Metering family).

Flow:
Step 1: Enter final micron reading before opening service valves.
Step 2: Enter standing decay test result (isolate pump, watch micron rise for 15 min).
Rule: Fail if reading > 500 microns after 15 min OR decay rate > 100 microns / 15 min.
Fail → escalate: "Standing vacuum decay indicates leak or moisture. Do not charge. Return to leak search or replace filter dryer."
Archive weight: 263 vacuum + 101 micron + 120 recovery = 484 combined.
🆕 Combustion Safety Check (Cross-Cutting)
⚠️ SAFETY🆕 NEW
Required safety check triggered by Not Heating + system age ≥ 12 years OR TESP > design budget OR flame roll-out reported.

Flow:
Step 1: Visual HX inspection photo (required — no skip).
Step 2: Optional combustion analyzer readings (CO ppm, O2%, flue temp).
Step 3: If visual crack OR CO > 100 ppm air-free → Card #21 Heat Exchanger Damage (red-tag).
Step 4: If clean visual + acceptable combustion → continue normal ignitor/flame sensor flow.
Archive weight: 87 HX + 37 CO + 86 combustion.
🔧 EXPAND: Not Cooling YES — 4-reading cascade
🆕 EXPANDTIER 1
Current live: 2-step cascade (suction PSI → conditional discharge PSI) → 4-card discrimination.

Expand to: 4-reading cascade adding superheat + subcool inputs.
Discrimination logic:
Low suction + high superheat + low subcool → Card #8 Refrigerant Leak (confirmed, not just hypothesis).
Low suction + low superheat + normal subcool → Card #15b/c TXV problem (bulb loss or hunting).
Normal pressures + high delta-T + high static → Card #20 Under-Airflow.
Normal pressures + normal delta-T + warm supply → Card #13 Ductwork Leak (current logic).
High discharge + normal subcool → Card #14 Dirty Coil.
High discharge + high subcool → Card #17 Overcharge (confirmed).
Any single-reading commit → downgrade confidence label to "hypothesis" until cascade completes.
Archive weight: AC Pressures class + Charging a TXV system with Probes + Class - What Superheat Signifies episodes are the specific source.
🔧 EXPAND: Making Noise — add "grinding" compressor path
🆕 EXPANDTIER 2
Current live: 5 noise routes (banging → compressor, hissing → suction, clicking → capacitor, grinding → contactor, squealing → blower).

Expand grinding: Grinding can also be compressor internal bearing failure, not just contactor. Add branch:
Grinding → Q: Location? [outdoor unit / indoor unit].
Outdoor → contactor photo (current) OR bearing test (amp draw pattern → Card #10b).
Indoor → blower motor bearing test (Card #4).
Archive weight: 468 compressor episodes cover bearing wear patterns extensively.
